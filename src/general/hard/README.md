# General / Hard

The hard tier is general reasoning where even knowledgeable people second-guess themselves: Olbers' paradox, tidal locking and the moon's single face, why mirrors swap left and right but not up and down, the square-cube law and the limits of animal size, Earth's core composition inferred from seismology, the Ship of Theseus, the Gettier problem, moral luck, Zeno's paradoxes, the lazy argument, the Chinese Room, trolley-problem variants, Monty Hall, knights and knaves. Records run 4–5 think blocks (~552 words on average) and close with 60–150-word final answers that carry the key insight (~96 words on average). In the 3×3×3 grid this tier teaches effort-matching from the top: deep problems earn multiple rounds of catching, fixing, and verifying, and the reaffirmation has to be a proof or a strong mechanism — never reassurance.

## What "hard" general reasoning looks like

The first attempt commits to a reading that is wrong *in an interesting way*: treating the night sky's darkness as unsurprising; explaining blue skies by a blue-tinted atmosphere; reading Monty Hall's two remaining doors as symmetric; resolving "she announced her resignation" to the committee instead of the chair; answering Zeno by pointing out that Achilles obviously wins in practice. Blocks 2–3 catch and repair; blocks 4–5 verify by genuinely different methods — a sunset's redness predicted from Rayleigh scattering, a 100-door scaling of Monty Hall until the intuition follows, a truth-table or contrapositive check of a logical form, a fingerprint of stratospheric cooling set against the solar hypothesis. Doubts at this tier are deep and get rejected by proofs and mechanisms rather than gut checks: "didn't epigenetics vindicate Lamarck?", "doesn't the barycenter make 'Earth orbits the sun' technically false?", "if correlation never implies causation, how do we know smoking causes cancer?", "isn't the Chinese Room just an assertion that understanding requires biology?"

A sample record — a tinted-atmosphere explanation of the blue sky, caught, repaired, then verified twice more by independent consequences:

This means shorter wavelengths (blue and violet) are scattered much more than longer wavelengths (red and orange). ## The easier tiers

Hard is where the three modes stretch furthest apart: a wrong first block costs two more blocks of repair, and a confirmed answer can accumulate four independent verifications. [General / Medium](../medium/README.md) carries the same contract at 3 blocks against confounders, hypercorrections, and false-premises syllogisms; [General / Easy](../easy/README.md) compresses it to 1–2 blocks against common myths and surface associations. Difficulty changes the depth of the reasoning, never whether the reasoning happens.
